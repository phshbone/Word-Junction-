# Word Junctions — brand direction

## Working name

**Word Junctions** (plural)

Reason: the product is about connections between two words and can continue into multiple connected words as the user explores.

## Core logo idea

The wordmark should visually behave like a network of pipe/junction connections rather than simply coloring random letters.

### Base treatment
- Primary letters: black / very dark ink.
- Accent letters and junction paths: muted gold.
- Accent concept should feel like pipe fittings, branching connections, and junction points rather than decorative highlights.

### Accent letters
Current proposed accent letters:
- **J** in Junctions
- **T** in Junctions
- **S** at the end of Junctions

These three letters should share the same gold color.

### J junction behavior
- The **J** should act as a true junction point.
- Two branches should emerge from it.
- One branch can travel upward into the upper wordmark and connect into the **W** area.
- The second branch can connect into another nearby letterform so the logo reads as a visible network, not a standalone J.
- The connection should resemble PVC / pipe-fitting geometry: clean, intentional bends and split points.

### T junction behavior
- The **T** should function like another branching junction.
- One arm can travel downward and connect visually toward the final **S** in Junctions.
- The other arm can travel upward and connect into the upper wordmark.
- The connected letters/path should use the same gold accent so the eye can trace the junction system.

### S behavior
- The final **S** in Junctions should be gold and act as an endpoint or continuation point for one of the T branches.

## Visual rule

Do not color every letter. Most of the wordmark should remain dark. Gold should be reserved for the letters and paths that actually participate in the junction system, so the color itself communicates connection.

## Open design question

The spoken concept included an upper connection described as going into the “R of Merge.” That exact destination needs to be resolved during logo sketching before it is treated as final. Preserve the structural idea — one branch of the T travels upward into the upper word — without locking the exact target letter yet.

## Design intent

The logo should make the name visually self-explanatory: one word can branch, connect, merge, and continue into another. The junctions should feel integrated into the typography, not like pipes drawn on top afterward.
